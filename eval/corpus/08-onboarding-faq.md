# New Joiner FAQ

The questions new people at Meridian Labs actually ask, in the order they usually
ask them. If your question is not here, ask it in `#new-joiners` on Wardroom and
we will add the answer.

## What arrives before my first day?

People Operations sends your welcome pack in Logbook **10 days before your start
date**. It contains your first-week schedule, your team's page, and the name of
your Onboarding Buddy.

Your laptop is shipped to arrive **at least two working days before day one** —
tell People Operations immediately if it has not, because the first day does not
work without it. Your email address and Compass invitation are activated the
evening before you start, not weeks ahead.

## What happens on day one?

A 45-minute welcome call with People Operations at **10:00 WET (Lisbon time)**,
then device setup with the IT Coordinator: Compass enrolment, both hardware
security keys, and Latchkey.

Nothing else is scheduled. You are not expected to read the whole handbook on day
one, and no one will ask you to introduce yourself to the whole company on a call.

## Who is my Onboarding Buddy and what do they do?

An engineer or peer from your own team, assigned before you start. They meet you
**daily for your first two weeks**, then weekly until day 90. They are the person
for questions you feel are too small to ask your manager — which is most of the
useful questions.

Your manager is separate: a weekly 30-minute one-to-one, starting in week one and
continuing indefinitely.

## What am I expected to have done, and by when?

- **Day 1** — laptop enrolled in Compass, both security keys registered, Latchkey
  set up.
- **Week 1** — security onboarding module completed, handbook skimmed, one-to-one
  scheduled with your manager.
- **Day 5** — your first pull request merged. It is meant to be small: a
  documentation fix or a test counts, and your Buddy will help you find one.
- **Day 30** — your first production deploy, with your Onboarding Buddy watching.
- **Day 90** — probation review with your manager and People Operations.

These are the company's obligations as much as yours. If day 5 arrives and
nobody has helped you merge anything, that is a failure of your team, not of you.

## How long is probation?

**90 days**, extendable once by a further 30 days, which happens rarely and never
as a surprise — an extension requires two documented one-to-ones raising the
concern beforehand.

## When am I paid, and where are my payslips?

Salary is paid on the **25th of each month**, or the preceding working day when
the 25th falls on a weekend. Your first month is pro-rated from your start date.

Payslips appear in Almanac **three working days before payday**. Expense claims
are paid in the same run — the expenses policy explains the cut-off.

## What equipment do I get?

A 14-inch laptop with 32 GB of memory, replaced every three years, plus two
hardware security keys.

A second monitor, keyboard, mouse, desk, and chair come out of your home-office
budget rather than from IT. The remote work policy sets the amount and the window
for claiming it, and you buy the items yourself and claim them back in Purser.

## Which meetings are actually recurring?

- **Team standup**, daily at 10:15 WET, 15 minutes. On Wednesdays it is written
  in your team channel instead, because Wednesdays are meeting-free.
- **All-hands**, Thursdays at 15:00 WET, 30 minutes, recorded.
- **Your one-to-one**, weekly, 30 minutes.

That is the entire standing calendar for most people. If your week fills up
beyond it, raise it at your one-to-one.

## Who do I ask about what?

- **People Operations Lead** — contract, leave, payroll, working from abroad.
- **Finance Partner** — expenses, travel bookings, company cards.
- **Security Lead** — access requests, devices, anything you would report to
  `#sec-report`.
- **Your Engineering Manager** — one-to-ones, growth, on-call readiness.
- **Hub Coordinator** — desks, building access, and the Lisbon door code.
- **Your Onboarding Buddy** — everything else, especially the small things.

## When will I stop feeling new?

Most people say the fog lifts somewhere between week six and week ten. The
company's own expectation is that you are independently productive by the end of
your first quarter, and the day-90 review is written on that basis rather than on
output in month one. Asking an obvious question in week nine is normal, and
asking it in public is better than asking it in a direct message.
