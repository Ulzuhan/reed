# Welcome to Meridian Labs

You have joined a company of 61 people that is trying to do one narrow thing
extremely well. This page explains what that thing is, how the company is put
together, and where to look when you need an answer. Everything else in the
handbook hangs off it.

## Who we are

Meridian Labs was founded in March 2019 by three data engineers who were tired
of guessing which dashboards would break when they renamed a column. We build
**Windrose**, a column-level data-lineage platform for analytics teams. As of
this revision, 143 customer organisations in 11 countries run Windrose against
their warehouses.

We closed a €14M Series A in June 2024, led by Northaven Capital, and have been
cash-flow positive since the third quarter of 2025. We are not in a hurry to
raise again.

We keep two hubs: the Lisbon head office on Rua do Alecrim, with 34 desks, and a
Tallinn office with 18. Neither is mandatory. Roughly half the company is fully
remote across nine countries.

## What we are trying to do

Our mission is to make the question *"what breaks if I change this column?"*
answerable in under a minute, by anyone, without asking a colleague.

That is not a slogan — it is the number we run the company on. Windrose reports a
median time-to-answer across all customer workspaces; the current figure is 41
seconds, against a target of 60. Every roadmap item is argued for on the basis of
what it does to that number or to the share of a warehouse we can map, which
stands at 88% of tables on a typical first connection.

## How we work

Three principles, each with a rule attached so you can tell whether we are
actually following them.

**Write it down.** Any decision that affects more than one team gets a one-page
memo in Logbook within two working days of being made. If it is not written
down, it did not happen, and you are entitled to reopen it.

**Small, reversible steps.** We would rather ship a change five times behind a
flag than once with a launch date. The mechanics are in the engineering
standards; the attitude is company-wide.

**Default to open.** Every page in Logbook is readable by every employee unless
it contains Tier 2 or higher data, as defined in the security and data handling
policy. Restricting a page requires a stated reason on the page itself.

## The rhythm of the year

We plan in 13-week quarters. The last week of each quarter is planning week: no
new feature work starts, and every team publishes its next-quarter plan in
Logbook by the Friday.

The all-hands runs every **Thursday at 15:00 WET (Lisbon time)** and is capped at
30 minutes. It is recorded, and the recording plus notes go up in Logbook within
24 hours, so attending live is never required.

**Wednesdays are no-meeting days.** Nothing recurring may be scheduled on a
Wednesday, and one-off invites need the recipient's agreement first.

Twice a year the whole company gathers for an onsite: four days in Lisbon each
March, and four days in Tallinn each September. Attendance is expected unless you are on leave,
and travel is booked under the expenses and travel policy.

## Where to find things

Our internal tools are all named after navigation instruments, a habit from the
founders' first office above a chandlery in Cais do Sodré. You will meet them in
your first week:

- **Logbook** — the wiki. This handbook, memos, runbooks, postmortems.
- **Almanac** — people system. Leave, working-abroad notices, desk booking, payslips.
- **Purser** — expenses and reimbursement claims.
- **Waypoint** — travel booking.
- **Wardroom** — chat. Channels over direct messages, always.
- **Compass** — single sign-on and device management.
- **Latchkey** — password manager. **Strongbox** — secrets and production access.
- **Slipway** — build and deployment pipeline.
- **Beacon** — paging and alerting. **Signalpost** — the public status page.

## Keeping this handbook honest

The handbook is owned by the People Operations Lead and reviewed twice a year, on
1 March and 1 September. This is revision 7, effective 1 March 2026.

Anyone may propose a change by opening a suggestion on the relevant Logbook page.
The owner of that policy must respond within 10 working days, in writing, either
accepting the change or explaining why not. Accepted changes are announced at the
next all-hands. A handbook nobody argues with is usually a handbook nobody reads.
